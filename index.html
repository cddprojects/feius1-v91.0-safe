Before you hit Apply

The checklist

Give the posting a closer look

Keep the listing open as you go. If something’s missing, here’s what to ask.

01 The posting

Is this job posting real?

Go to the company’s careers page and look for the same job title, location, and job ID if there is one. If you don’t see the opening, contact the company using the number or email on its website.

If someone asks you to pay to get hired, or sends a check and asks you to send some of the money back, walk away.

What to ask

“Is this job still open? Where should I apply?”

  • How to spot a job scam (FTC) (opens in a new tab)
  • Where to look for jobs (USAGov) (opens in a new tab)

02 The pay

What does it pay?

Look for an hourly rate, a salary range, or a flat fee for the project. If the ad says “up to $25 an hour,” ask how much is base pay and how much depends on tips, commissions, or bonuses.

If there’s no pay range, your state labor office can help you find out which employers have to list one.

What to ask

“What’s the starting pay for this job at this location?”

  • Find your state labor office (opens in a new tab)
  • New York’s pay disclosure rules (opens in a new tab)

03 The hours

What hours would I work?

Get the days, shift times, and usual hours per week. “Flexible schedule” could mean flexible work you pick up, or that your manager needs you to cover different shifts each week. Check when training happens and confirm the time zone if you’ll work remotely.

What to ask

“What would a typical week look like? When would I need to be available for training?”

04 The location

Where would I work?

For on-site or hybrid jobs, get the exact address and ask how often you’ll need to go in. For remote jobs and online jobs, check whether the company hires in your state and whether you’ll need to show up in person for training or meetings.

What to ask

“Can I do this job from [your state]? Is there anything I’d need to come in for?”

U.S. job market · November 2026

October was a stronger month for jobs

U.S. employers added 165,000 jobs to their payrolls in October, well above the monthly average of 48,000 over the previous year. The unemployment rate was little changed at 4.1%.

October jobs report from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (opens in a new tab)

Where employers added jobs

Jobs added minus jobs lost in October 2026

+70,000

Professional and business services

Technical services and administrative support both added jobs. If you’re looking for office work, try “administrative assistant” or “operations assistant.”

+42,000

Health care

Hospitals, home health agencies, and outpatient centers added jobs. Check their careers pages for patient scheduler, front desk, or home health aide openings that match your qualifications.

+25,000

Transportation and warehousing

Courier services accounted for most of the increase. If you need shift work, search for “courier” or “package handler” near you and check the hours.

What this means for your search

  • Hotels and restaurants are still worth a look. Leisure and hospitality employment barely moved in October. In September, hotels and food service businesses brought on more people and saw more people leave. Check local employers for openings even when the industry’s job count is flat.
  • Look at openings and hires together. Employers had 7.5 million jobs open at the end of September and hired 5.4 million people during the month. Both figures changed little from August. For openings you can apply to today, check company careers pages in your area.
  • Ask how many hours you can count on. In October, 4.8 million people were working part time because their hours had been cut or they couldn’t find full-time work. That was up 339,000 from September. If you need a steady paycheck, ask whether the employer guarantees a minimum number of hours.

September openings and hires report (BLS) (opens in a new tab)

Take the search local

Pick a job title and search for it with your city or ZIP code. If you’re working around another job and want a second income, add “part time,” “remote,” “evenings,” or “weekends.” Use the checklist on any listing that looks promising.

U.S. job search updates

New rules and scam alerts

Check the date and the place on each note below before you compare a job posting or an offer.

California and Washington

Starts January 1, 2027

Minimum wage goes up in January

Starting January 1, the statewide minimum wage is set to rise to $17.40 an hour in California and $17.73 an hour in Washington. If you’re comparing offers for 2027, check the rate where you’ll work. Some cities and counties set a higher minimum.

  • California labor department announcement (opens in a new tab)
  • Washington labor department announcement (opens in a new tab)

Maine and Virginia

In effect since July 2026

More job ads have to show what they pay

Since July 29, Maine employers with 10 or more employees have had to list the expected pay range in covered job ads or say when a job pays commission only. Since July 1, Virginia employers have had to list the wage, salary, or pay range in covered job ads. They also can’t ask applicants about past pay.

If the ad is vague, ask what pay range the company has in mind.

  • Maine Department of Labor update (opens in a new tab)
  • Virginia pay disclosure law (opens in a new tab)

Chicago, Illinois

In effect since July 1, 2026

More Chicago workers may qualify for advance notice of shifts

Chicago’s Fair Workweek rules can apply to workers making up to $33.85 an hour or $64,945.55 a year. Coverage also depends on the industry and employer. If you’re applying for shift work in Chicago, ask if the job is covered and when you’ll get your schedule.

  • Chicago Fair Workweek coverage (opens in a new tab)
  • Chicago’s July 2026 labor updates (opens in a new tab)

Nationwide

FTC alert · April 2026

Watch for texts about remote jobs you never applied for

The FTC flagged messages that pitch a vague remote job and ask you to text back “YES” or “INTERESTED.” Skip the reply. If you want to see whether the opening is real, go straight to the company’s careers page or call the number on its website.
